# Form standards behind the exercise files

What each exercise in `public/exercises/` asks for, where the standard came from, which mistakes the guidance names and how the coach handles each, and what the camera is held to. The measures and doses below are generated from the files themselves (`node` with `Spec.words`), so they say what the coach does. The numbers are starting points chosen from published guidance and checked against real photos; the studio (review.html) tunes them against real takes, and the file is where the tuned numbers go.

Some rules run through all of them:

- **What the camera can measure** is an angle between three landmarks, a line against vertical or the floor, a height or an offset as a share of a limb, or a length against its own start. Words like "tighten the thigh" or "keep the hips stacked" have no number, so they go into the how-to and into *what the camera cannot see*, not into a fault.
- **Order of correction** is the chain of cause: whatever the rest of the body stands on is said first. Faults judged at rest (`when: always` or `between`) are coached before the movement is asked for.
- **Readings against the start.** Where a person's own shape or the model's habits put a steady offset into a reading — the model sets a flat foot's heel above the sole; arms longer than thighs tilt a table-top; a trunk line is never quite level — the reading is taken as its change from the start position (`fromStart`), not against a fixed number. This replaced the glute bridge's hand-set `footBias`.
- **Which way the body faces** comes from the feet when standing or sitting (heel to toe): an upright trunk line cannot also say which way is forward.
- **Joint angles that go past straight** (the bridge's and the donkey kick's hip) are read on past 180, so a movement pushed too far is not taken for one coming back.
- **Sources** were read as search-result excerpts in this pass (the health sites refused direct fetches); where a source is named without a link, the excerpt did not give one. The clamshell, side-lying and inner-thigh raises and the donkey kick rest on common clinical guidance rather than a source read in this pass.

## Shoulder

### Pendulum swing (`pendulum`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 30 s hold.

**What the guidance asks**

- Good hand on a table, one foot forward, knees soft, bent forward from the hips; the stiff arm hangs straight and relaxed.
- Shift the body weight so the arm swings in small circles (about a foot across) — the shoulder muscles do nothing.
- How far to lean varies by source (“slightly” to 75–90°). 10 circles each way; widen over the weeks.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Swinging the arm with the shoulder muscles | ‘Arm lifting’ — “Let the arm hang — swing it with your body, not your shoulder” (large swings only) |
| Circles too big too early | ‘Arm lifting’ — “Let the arm hang — swing it with your body, not your shoulder” (23°, a circle about half a metre) |
| Too upright — the arm rubs the body | ‘Lean further’ — “Lean forward a little more, hand on the table — the arm has to hang clear” |
| Elbow bent, arm held | ‘Elbow bending’ — “Let the arm hang straight” |

**What the camera is held to**

- In the hold, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be between 30° and 95°.
- In the hold, the arm line (shoulder to wrist) lifted from hanging must be at most 23°.
- In the hold, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at least 150°.

*It cannot see:* Whether the shoulder muscles are truly relaxed, and the side-to-side part of the swing, which is toward the camera: it sees the lean, the arm’s forward-and-back swing and the elbow. The arm should feel heavy, like a rope.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Lean band widened to 30–95° (the old “about sixty” called correct deep leans too far forward).
- Arm swing at most 23° (35° allowed a circle 75 cm across).
- Words: one foot forward, knees soft; the side-to-side swing is not measured.

**Sources**

- [Harvard Health — Stretching exercises for frozen shoulder](https://www.health.harvard.edu/stretching-exercises-frozen-shoulder)
- [AAOS OrthoInfo — Rotator cuff and shoulder conditioning program](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/)
- [Merck Manual — Codman exercises](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/multimedia/image/codman-exercises)
- [HealthLinkBC — Pendulum swing exercise](https://www.healthlinkbc.ca/healthwise/pendulum-swing-exercise-shoulder)
- [Long et al. 2010, pendulum circle size and muscle activity (JOSPT)](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2010.3095)

### Wall walk (`wallwalk`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 3 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Face the wall about three-quarters of an arm’s length away, fingertips on it at waist height, elbow slightly bent.
- Walk the fingers up as far as is comfortable — the fingers do the work; hold 3–5 s; walk down, helped by the other hand.
- Don’t lean back or hike the shoulder.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Leaning back to gain height | ‘Leaning back’ — “Don’t lean back — stand tall and let the shoulder do the reach” |
| Shrugging | ‘Shoulder shrugging’ — “Shoulder down, away from the ear” |
| Elbow bent at the top | ‘Elbow bending’ — “Keep the arm long — elbow nearly straight” (judged at the top only — a bend on the way up is geometry) |
| Leaning into the wall | ‘Leaning in’ — “Stand tall — don’t lean into the wall” (allowed up to 20°; sources split) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the shoulder angle (hip–shoulder–wrist) must be at least 110°.
- Through the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be between -12° and 20°.
- At the top of the rep, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at least 125°.
- Through the rep, the ear-to-shoulder distance, as % of the trunk, as % of its start, must be at least 78%.
- The rep is under way once the shoulder angle (hip–shoulder–wrist) rises past 90° and counts once it is back under 40°.

*It cannot see:* Whether the fingers or the shoulder muscles are doing the lifting, and whether the shoulder blade is moving well: it sees how high the arm goes, the trunk, the elbow and the shoulder rising toward the ear.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Elbow judged at the top only, at 125°.
- Lean allowed −12° back / 20° in; shrug at 78% of the start; reach target 110° (frozen-stage average flexion is about 117°).
- Hold 3 s; distance words fixed.

**Sources**

- [Harvard Health — Stretching exercises for frozen shoulder](https://www.health.harvard.edu/stretching-exercises-frozen-shoulder)
- [Physitrack — Shoulder wall walks](https://us.physitrack.com/home-exercise-video/shoulder-wall-walks)
- [Rehab Hero — Wall crawl](https://www.rehabhero.ca/exercise/wall-crawl-1)
- [Frozen shoulder range norms (PMC3096148)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3096148/)

### Arm raise to the side (`abduction`) — facing the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 2 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Standing facing the camera, arm by the side, palm forward and thumb pointing out; the thumb turns up as the arm rises.
- Raise slowly out to the side with the elbow straight and the shoulder blade set; lower slowly.
- Strengthening versions stop at shoulder height (90°); mobility versions go as high as is comfortable. 2–3 sets of 10–15.
- A little shoulder rise past 90° is normal (scapulohumeral rhythm); a hike toward the ear is the cheat.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Shoulder hiking toward the ear | ‘Shoulder hiking’ — “Shoulder down — don’t hike it toward the ear” |
| Leaning the trunk away from the arm to look higher | ‘Leaning away’ — “Trunk upright — don’t lean away from the arm” |
| Bending the elbow | ‘Elbow bending’ — “Keep the arm straight” (only partly visible: with the thumb up the forearm bends toward the camera) |
| Swinging the arm up | *not seen by the camera:* not judged (only the lowering is timed) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the shoulder angle (hip–shoulder–wrist) must be between 80° and 110°.
- Through the rep, the shoulder over the other shoulder (the line between them off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 8°.
- Through the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright, as the change since the start, must be within 7° of the start.
- Through the rep, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at least 150°.
- The rep is under way once the shoulder angle (hip–shoulder–wrist) rises past 45° and counts once it is back under 25°.

*It cannot see:* Whether the shoulder blade is turning as it should, and an elbow that bends toward the phone: with the thumb up the forearm bends toward the camera, which a front view barely sees. It sees the arm’s angle, the shoulder rising and the trunk leaning.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- New check: the trunk leaning away from the arm, against the start.
- The shrug is read against the start, so a resting tilt of your own is not a shrug.
- Outward is measured from the other shoulder to this one; how-to and start words fixed (palm forward, thumb out).

**Sources**

- [JOI — Thumbs-up shoulder exercises](https://www.joionline.net/library/thumbs_up_shoulder_exercises/)
- [Vissco — Standing shoulder abduction](https://www.vissco.com/physiotherapy-exercise/standing-shoulder-abduction/)
- [Physiopedia — Scapulohumeral rhythm](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Scapulohumeral_Rhythm)
- [Frozen shoulder range norms (PMC3096148)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3096148/)

### Shoulder turn-out (`extrot`) — facing the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 5 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Elbow bent to 90° and kept at the side (a rolled towel between elbow and ribs helps); forearm forward, thumb up.
- Turn the forearm outward without the elbow leaving the side; hold 5 s; return slowly.
- Frozen shoulders: out only a few inches (2–3 in ≈ 10°) at first; 10–15 reps.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Elbow drifting away from the side | ‘Elbow leaving the side’ — “Keep the elbow tucked in at your side” (sideways drift; forward/back is not visible) |
| Elbow straightening, forearm dropping | ‘Elbow opening’ — “Keep the elbow bent to a right angle” |
| Turning the trunk | ‘Body turning’ — “Keep the chest facing the phone — turn the arm, not your body” |
| Shrugging | ‘Shoulder hiking’ — “Shoulder down — turn the arm, don’t lift the shoulder” |
| Snapping back | lowering timed |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, how far the wrist is outward of the elbow, as % of the upper arm, must be at least 25%.
- Through the rep, the shoulder angle (hip–shoulder–wrist), as the change since the start, must be at most 15°.
- Through the rep, the shoulder over the other shoulder (the line between them off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 7°.
- Through the rep, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at most 120°.
- Through the rep, the shoulder width, as % of the pelvis, as % of its start, must be at least 85%.
- The rep is under way once how far the wrist is outward of the elbow, as % of the upper arm rises past 8% and counts once it is back under 4%.

*It cannot see:* The shoulder joint itself, and how bent the elbow is while the hand points at the phone: it reads the turn from how far the hand moves out from the elbow, the elbow swinging away from the side, the shoulder lifting and the chest turning. Keep the elbow pinned yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- The turn is now the wrist’s outward offset from the elbow (0 at rest, signed): the old forearm-length reading read 24–34 at rest and could not tell in from out.
- Elbow bend judged only for opening out (foreshortening made a bent elbow unreadable).
- Elbow-off-the-side read as the hip–shoulder–elbow angle against the start, so a towel roll is fine; new shrug check; hold 5 s.

**Sources**

- [Harvard Health — Stretching exercises for frozen shoulder](https://www.health.harvard.edu/stretching-exercises-frozen-shoulder)
- [East Sussex Healthcare NHS — shoulder exercise leaflet](https://www.esht.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/0633.pdf)
- [North Cheshire and Mersey NHS — Shoulder range of motion exercises](https://northcheshireandmersey.nhs.uk/media/luzaxp34/shoulder-range-of-motion-exercises-patient-advice-sheet.pdf)
- [Reinold et al. 2004, towel roll in external rotation (JOSPT)](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2004.34.7.385)
- [E3 Rehab — Rotator cuff exercises](https://e3rehab.com/rotator-cuff-exercises/)
- [Frozen shoulder range norms (PMC3096148)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3096148/)

### Cross-body stretch (`crossbody`) — facing the phone

Default dose: 3 × 20 s hold.

**What the guidance asks**

- Standing tall, chest facing forward, shoulders relaxed; lift the arm to chest height and bring it across the body.
- The other hand draws it further across at the upper arm, until a stretch is felt at the back of the shoulder (AAOS: hold the upper arm, not the elbow joint).
- Don’t turn the body. Holds of 15–30 s; AAOS: 30 s × 4 per side.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Turning the trunk instead of drawing the arm across | ‘Body turning’ — “Keep the chest facing the phone — draw the arm across, don’t turn” |
| Shoulder hiking under the pull | ‘Shoulder hiking’ — “Shoulder down — level with the other” |
| Side-bending | ‘Side-bending’ — “Stay upright — don’t lean to the side” |
| Not far enough across | ‘Draw further’ — “Draw the arm further across your chest” (now the elbow’s travel across) |
| Pulling on the forearm or pressing the elbow joint | *not seen by the camera:* words only — not visible |

**What the camera is held to**

- In the hold, how far the elbow is outward of the shoulder, as % of the shoulders, must be at least 40%.
- In the hold, the shoulder over the other shoulder (the line between them off level), as the change since the start, must be within 10° of the start.
- In the hold, the shoulder width, as % of the pelvis, as % of its start, must be at least 85%.

*It cannot see:* How strong the stretch feels, and where your hand holds — keep it on the upper arm, not the forearm or the elbow joint. It sees how far across the elbow is, the shoulders level and the chest facing the phone; a turn of the whole body is not seen.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- “How far across” is now the elbow’s travel from its own shoulder toward the other (the old wrist-to-far-shoulder reading tracked arm height, not the stretch).
- Shoulder level is read against the start; “dropped” relabelled as side-bending.
- Chest-facing threshold 85%.

**Sources**

- [AAOS OrthoInfo — Rotator cuff and shoulder conditioning program](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/rotator-cuff-and-shoulder-conditioning-program/)
- [Mayo Clinic — Stretching](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/stretching/art-20546848)
- [Harvard Health — Stretching exercises for frozen shoulder](https://www.health.harvard.edu/stretching-exercises-frozen-shoulder)
- [Cleveland Clinic — Frozen shoulder exercises](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/frozen-shoulder-exercises)
- [Wilk et al. 2013, stabilised cross-body stretch (JOSPT)](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2013.4990)

## Knee and hip

### Wall sit (`wallsit`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 60 s hold.

**What the guidance asks**

- Back flat on the wall, feet hip-width, a step out; slide down to thighs level, knees over the ankles.
- Hold, breathing. Rehab versions are shallower (20–30° of bend for kneecap pain).

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Feet too close (knees past the toes) | ‘Feet behind knees’ — “Bring your feet forward” |
| Feet too far out | ‘Feet ahead of knees’ — “Bring your feet back” |
| Too high / too deep | ‘Too high’ — “Lower down”; ‘Too deep’ — “Come up a little” |
| Leaning off the wall | ‘Leaning forward’ — “Press your back flat to the wall” |

**What the camera is held to**

- In the hold, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be between 85° and 110°.
- In the hold, the shin line (knee to ankle) from the floor must be between 80° and 100°.
- In the hold, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be within 12° of upright.

*It cannot see:* Whether your lower back is pressed flat: it sees the line from hip to shoulder, not the curve between. Weight through the heels is yours to feel.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Shin read to the ankle (the heel sits 5–7 cm behind it, so a knee over the ankle read as feet behind the knees).
- Shin band ±10 (±5 was the model’s own error and stuttered the clock).

**Sources**

- [ISSA — Wall sit](https://www.issaonline.com/blog/post/wall-sits)
- [MyHealth Alberta — Wall squat for patellofemoral pain (bo1601)](https://myhealth.alberta.ca/Health/aftercareinformation/pages/conditions.aspx?hwid=bo1601)

### Knee raise (`kneeraise`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 10 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Standing tall holding a chair; raise one knee until the thigh is level (or as high as is comfortable).
- The trunk stays tall — no leaning back; lower with control.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Leaning back to lift the knee | ‘Leaning back’ — “Stand tall — don't lean back” |
| Knee not coming up | ‘Knee low’ — “Lift the knee a little higher — thigh level, if comfortable” |
| Foot swung out or tucked | ‘Foot out in front’ — “Let the lower leg hang — foot under the knee”; ‘Foot tucked back’ — “Let the foot hang under the knee” (the lower leg off plumb) |
| Foot position | *not seen by the camera:* not judged — no source sets one |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the shin line (ankle to knee) from upright must be within 20° of upright.
- At the top of the rep, the thigh line (hip to knee) lifted from hanging must be at least 75°.
- At the top of the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be between -8° and 15°.
- The rep is under way once the thigh line (hip to knee) lifted from hanging rises past 45° and counts once it is back under 20°.

*It cannot see:* The standing side and the hips staying level: it follows the raised leg nearest the camera and the trunk. Balance is yours.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- The thigh’s height is judged directly (the knee angle stood in for it and said “bend your knee” when the fix was to lift higher).
- New trunk check; the foot is no longer judged.

**Sources**

- [University of Melbourne CHESM — Hip flexor exercises](https://healthsciences.unimelb.edu.au/departments/physiotherapy/chesm/video-library/exercise/hip-oa/hip-flexor-exercises)
- [Versus Arthritis — Exercises for the knees](https://www.versusarthritis.org/about-arthritis/exercising-with-arthritis/exercises-for-healthy-joints/knee-exercises/)

### Straight leg raise (`slr`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 5 s at the top, lowering over 2 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Lying on the back; most clinics bend the other knee with the foot flat (it keeps the back flat). Tighten the thigh, toes up.
- Lift the straight leg until the heel is about 10–20 cm up — never past 45°, the height of the other knee. Hold about 5 s.
- Lower slowly with the knee straight.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Knee bending (quad lag) | ‘Knee bending’ — “Straighten your knee — tighten the thigh” |
| Lifting past 45° | ‘Too high’ — “Not so high — forty five degrees at most” |
| Toes pointing away | ‘Toes pointing away’ — “Pull your toes up toward you” |
| The other leg lifting | ‘Other leg lifting’ — “Keep the other foot on the floor” |
| Back arching | *not seen by the camera:* words only |
| Swinging up | lowering timed (2 s) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At all times, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at least 165°.
- At the top of the rep, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), as the change from rest, must be between 15° and 45°.
- Through the rep, the height of the other heel over the hip, as % of the other shin, as the change since the start, must be at most 15%.
- At all times, the ankle angle at the heel (toe–heel–knee) must be between 60° and 105°.
- The rep is under way once the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), as the change from rest rises past 10° and counts once it is back under 5°.

*It cannot see:* Whether the lower back is flat on the floor, the leg rolling out, and whether the thigh is truly tight: it sees the knee angle, the lift, the toes and the other foot. Press the back down yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- The lift is read from the working leg against its own start, so the other knee may be bent; band 15–45° (no source sets a 30° minimum).
- The other leg is judged on its heel staying down (straight or bent), not on staying straight.
- The working leg is picked as the straight one lifted (a bent knee sits higher); hold 5 s, lower over 2.

**Sources**

- [NHS South Tees — Straight leg raise](https://www.southtees.nhs.uk/resources/straight-leg-raise/)
- [AAOS OrthoInfo — Knee conditioning program](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/)

### Prone leg raise (`proneraise`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 3 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Face down, forehead on the hands, legs straight.
- Tighten the buttock and lift one straight leg a little — a hand’s width at the foot, about 10° at the hip; hip bones on the floor; hold 3–5 s.
- No arching, no rolling the pelvis.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Hip lifting / pelvis rolling | ‘Hip lifting’ — “Hips down on the floor — lift the leg, not the hip” |
| Chest lifting, back arching | ‘Chest lifting’ — “Chest down — forehead on your hands” |
| Knee bending | ‘Knee bending’ — “Keep the knee straight” |
| Too high | ‘Too high’ — “Not so high — a hand's width at the foot” |
| Toe position | *not seen by the camera:* not judged — sources disagree |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), as the change from rest, must be between -12° and -3°.
- At all times, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at least 155°.
- Through the rep, the hip over the shoulder (the trunk line off level), as the change since the start, must be between -5° and 4°.
- The rep is under way once the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), as the change from rest falls under -2° and counts once it is back over 0°.

*It cannot see:* The pelvis rolling toward the camera and the lower back muscles taking over: it sees the lift, the knee, the hip rising and the chest lifting. Lift from the buttock, not the back.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Lift read at the knee (a bending knee no longer counts as a lift); band 3–12° (the old 0–5 could not call a lift short).
- Hip and chest read against the start; knee at 155; the leg measured is the one whose hip opens (a small lift never moved the “higher knee” pick).
- Hold 3 s.

**Sources**

- [AAOS OrthoInfo — Knee conditioning program](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/recovery/knee-conditioning-program/)
- [Wellen — Prone hip extension](https://www.getwellen.com/exercises/prone-hip-extension)
- [Prone hip extension and the lumbar spine (JOSPT 2007)](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2007.2435)

### Forward lunge (`lunge`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 1 s at the top.

**What the guidance asks**

- A long step forward, landing heel first; lower straight down to a front knee of about 90°.
- Front knee over the ankle, in line with the second toe; back knee just above the floor; trunk upright.
- Push back through the front heel.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Front knee well ahead of the foot | ‘Knee too far forward’ — “Take a longer step — knee over the ankle” |
| Front heel lifting (step too short) | ‘Front heel lifting’ — “Front heel down — take a longer step” |
| Leaning forward | ‘Leaning forward’ — “Chest up — stay tall” |
| Not low enough | ‘Not low enough’ — “Lower until the front thigh is level” |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be between 80° and 100°.
- At the top of the rep, the shin line (ankle to knee) from upright must be at most 20°.
- At the top of the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be within 15° of upright.
- At the top of the rep, the foot line off the floor (heel over toe), as the change since the start, must be at most 10°.
- The rep is under way once the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) falls under 120° and counts once it is back over 160°.

*It cannot see:* The back knee and the front knee caving inward: it judges the leg nearest the phone — its knee angle, its shin against vertical — and the trunk. Take a long enough step yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- “Knee past the ankle” relabelled “knee too far forward” (20° of shin lean is the knee over the ball of the foot).
- New check: the front heel lifting.

**Sources**

- [Cleveland Clinic — How to do lunges](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/lunges-muscles-worked)
- [ACE Exercise Library — Forward lunge](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/94/forward-lunge/)
- [Farrokhi et al. 2008, trunk position in the lunge (JOSPT)](https://www.jospt.org/doi/10.2519/jospt.2008.2634)

### Inner range quads (`quadset`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 5 s at the top, lowering over 2 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Lying with a roll (towel, foam roller, about 15 cm) under the knee so it is bent over it.
- Pull the toes back and straighten the knee, the heel lifting, the back of the knee staying on the roll; hold 5 s.
- Lower slowly; relax before the next.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Thigh lifting off the roll (the whole leg from the hip) | ‘Thigh lifting’ — “Keep the back of the knee on the roll” |
| Not straightening fully | ‘Knee not straight’ — “Straighten the knee fully — the leg in one line” |
| Toes not pulled up | ‘Toes not pulled up’ — “Pull your toes toward you” |
| Roll too small | ‘Prop the knee higher’ — “Prop the knee higher — a rolled towel or a foam roller under it” (between reps) |
| Dropping the heel | lowering timed (2 s) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at least 170°.
- Between reps, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at most 160°.
- Through the rep, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), as the change since the start, must be at most 6°.
- At the top of the rep, the ankle angle at the heel (toe–heel–knee) must be at most 100°.
- The rep is under way once the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) rises past 165° and counts once it is back under 160°.

*It cannot see:* How hard the thigh is working: it sees the knee straighten, the thigh stay on the roll and the toes come up, which go with the contraction. Press as hard as is comfortable.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Renamed “Inner range quads” (NHS name; “static quads” is the flat-leg version).
- New check: the thigh staying on the roll, against the start.
- Straight at 170 (175 was inside the model’s noise); the rep starts at 165 so a not-quite-straight knee is told.

### Seated knee extension (`kneeext`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 5 s at the top, lowering over 2 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Sitting tall, feet flat, thighs supported on the seat.
- Toes up and straighten the knee fully; hold about 5 s; lower slowly.
- The thigh stays on the seat; the trunk doesn’t lean back to help.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Not straightening fully | ‘Not fully straight’ — “Straighten your knee fully” |
| Thigh lifting off the seat | ‘Thigh lifting’ — “Keep your thigh on the seat — straighten from the knee” |

| Swinging the leg up and dropping it | lowering timed (2 s) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee over the hip (the thigh line off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 8°.

- Through the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright, as the change since the start, must be at least -8°.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Renamed “Seated knee extension”.
- Thigh lift read against the start (the chair’s height moved the old absolute angle).

### Step-up (`stepup`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 1 s at the top, lowering over 2 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Start on the bottom stair or a 10–20 cm step; go higher as it gets easy, no higher than mid-shin.
- Whole foot on the step; push through it and stand fully tall; the back foot only follows.
- Step down slowly (stepping down loads the kneecap more).

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Pushing off the back foot / throwing the trunk at the step | ‘Throwing forward’ — “Chest up — push with the leg on the step” |
| Not standing fully tall | ‘Knee not straight’ — “Stand up tall — straighten the knee”; ‘Hips not through’ — “Stand tall — hips forward”; ‘Leaning forward’ — “Stand tall at the top” |
| Knee well past the foot (step too high) | ‘Knee past the toes’ — “Knee back over your foot — whole foot on the step” |
| Dropping down | lowering timed (2 s) |
| Knee caving in | *not seen by the camera:* words only |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at least 165°.
- At the top of the rep, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder) must be at least 160°.
- At the top of the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be within 15° of upright.
- At all times, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be at most 30°.
- At all times, the shin line (ankle to knee) from upright must be at most 30°.
- The rep is under way once the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) rises past 155° and counts once it is back under 145°.

*It cannot see:* The knee caving inward and the pelvis dropping: it judges the stepping leg from the side — its knee and hip at the top, its shin at the start — and the trunk. Keep the knee over the second toe yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Step-height words now start low (the old “right angle at the knee” needs a 25–30 cm step).
- Forward lean allowed up to 30° in the push, 15° at the top (20° “always” called a normal push-off).
- Return counted at 145 so a low step resets; lower over 2 s.

### Heel slide (`heelslide`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 5 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Lying on the back, legs straight; slide the heel toward the buttock, bending the knee as far as is comfortable, heel on the floor.
- Hold 5–10 s at the deepest point; kneecap to the ceiling, hips level; slide back out.
- Range goals after knee replacement: 90° early, 110–120° by about six weeks.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Heel lifting off the floor | ‘Heel lifting’ — “Keep the heel on the floor as it slides” |
| Hip lifting to help | ‘Hip lifting’ — “Keep the hips on the floor” |

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

### Mini squat (`minisquat`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 2 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Feet hip-width, toes forward, a chair to hold; sit the hips back and bend the knees about 45° (a quarter of the way down).
- Knees over the second toes, heels down, chest up; hold 2–5 s.
- NHS knee-arthritis leaflets stop at about 45° of bend.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Going too deep | ‘Too deep’ — “Not so deep — a quarter bend is enough” |
| Knees travelling well past the toes | ‘Knees well forward’ — “Sit back a little — hips back, heels down” (a load cue: “sit back a little”) |
| Heels lifting | ‘Heels lifting’ — “Heels down” |
| Chest falling forward | ‘Chest falling’ — “Chest up” |
| Knees caving in | *not seen by the camera:* words only (front view needed) |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be between 130° and 150°.
- Through the rep, how far the knee is forward of the toes, as % of the shin, must be at most 10%.
- Through the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be at most 35°.
- Through the rep, the foot line off the floor (heel over toe), as the change since the start, must be at most 8°.
- The rep is under way once the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) falls under 165° and counts once it is back over 172°.

*It cannot see:* The knees collapsing inward and the weight shifting to one leg: it sees the knee bend from the side, the shin lean, the trunk and the heels. Keep the knees over the second toes yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Which way the body faces comes from the feet (standing, the trunk line was a coin toss).
- Knee past the toe tips measured directly (at most a tenth of the shin).
- Heel read against the start; depth to 130°.

### Sit to stand (`sitstand`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 1 s at the top, lowering over 3 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- A firm chair (about 43 cm), arms folded on the chest, feet hip-width and just behind the knees.
- Lean forward — nose over toes — and stand fully up without the hands; sit down slowly (2–5 s).

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Feet too far forward | ‘Feet too far forward’ — “Feet back a little — just behind the knees” (judged sitting) |
| Heels lifting | ‘Heels lifting’ — “Heels down — push through the whole foot” |
| Not standing fully up | ‘Not fully up’ — “Stand all the way up — straighten the knees”; ‘Still leaning’ — “Stand tall at the top” |
| Dropping into the seat | lowering timed (3 s) |
| Pushing with the hands | *not seen by the camera:* words only |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be at least 165°.

- Between reps, the shin line (ankle to knee) from the floor must be at least 85°.
- At all times, the foot line off the floor (heel over toe), as the change since the start, must be at most 10°.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Facing from the feet (sitting upright, the trunk line could not say which way the body faced).

### Donkey kick (`donkeykick`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 2 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Hands under shoulders, knees under hips, back flat, neck long.
- Knee bent at 90°, sole to the ceiling; lift until the thigh is in line with the back; hold 1–2 s; no arching or rocking.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Arching to lift higher | ‘Thigh above back’ — “Not so high — thigh in line with your back” |
| Rocking the trunk | ‘Rocking back’ — “Keep your trunk still — weight over hands and knees”; ‘Rocking forward’ — “Keep your trunk still” |
| Knee straightening | ‘Knee straightening’ — “Keep the knee bent — sole to the ceiling” |
| Shoulders behind the wrists | ‘Shoulders behind wrists’ — “Shoulders forward over your wrists” |
| Hips rolling open | *not seen by the camera:* words only |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be between 80° and 100°.
- At the top of the rep, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), read on past 180, must be at least 165°.

- At all times, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at least 165°.
- Through the rep, the shoulder over the hip (the trunk line off level), as the change since the start, must be within 8° of the start.
- The rep is under way once the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder), read on past 180 rises past 125° and counts once it is back under 112°.

*It cannot see:* The shape of the spine — a sag or a round — the hips rolling open toward the working side, and the far leg: it judges the arms, the trunk staying still and the lifted leg from the side. Keep your weight centred and your hips square yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- The trunk is judged for staying still, against the start (a long-armed table-top has the shoulders 10–20° above the hips, and “back sagging” fired at the start).
- Thigh up to 8° above the back; start line at 112 (a table-top reads 100–115 at the hip); the lift read on past straight.

- The rep is under way once the thigh line (hip to knee) lifted from hanging rises past 45° and counts once it is back under 20°.

*It cannot see:* The hips rotating, the lower back arching and the head: it sees the leg and arm heights, the reaching knee and the hip-to-shoulder line from the side. Keep both hip bones pointing at the floor and your eyes to the floor yourself.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Leg at most 95° from hanging; new straight-leg check.
- “Back sagging/arching” renamed to what the camera sees (chest dropping, rocking back) — there is no landmark on the lower back.
- Hold 6 s; the “harder” option is now the McGill sweep, not elbow-to-knee.

**Sources**

- [McGill — Enhancing low back health through stabilization exercise](https://www.backfitpro.com/)
- [Harvard Health — Bird dog](https://www.health.harvard.edu/bird-dog)
- [Mayo Clinic — Core-strength exercises](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/core-strength/art-20546851)
- [ACE Exercise Library — Bird dog](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/14/bird-dog/)

### Prone press-up (`pressup`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 1 × 10 reps, 2 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- McKenzie extension in lying: face down, hands under the shoulders, press the top half up as far as pain permits.
- Pelvis and legs relaxed on the floor so the low back sags; hold 1–2 s; lower; ten reps, repeated through the day.
- Pain moving toward the spine is fine; pain spreading down the leg means stop.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Hips lifting | ‘Hips lifting’ — “Keep the hips on the floor — let the back sag” |
| Legs lifting / back muscles doing it | ‘Legs lifting’ — “Legs relaxed on the floor — the arms do the work” |
| Pressing short | ‘Press short’ — “Press up a little higher — only as far as is comfortable” (20°, as far as comfortable) |
| Pushing into spreading pain | *not seen by the camera:* words only |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the shoulder over the hip (the trunk line off level) must be at least 20°.
- Through the rep, the hip over the knee (the thigh line off level), as the change since the start, must be between -8° and 5°.
- The rep is under way once the shoulder over the hip (the trunk line off level) rises past 15° and counts once it is back under 8°.

*It cannot see:* Whether the back muscles are relaxed and where the pain is moving: it sees the chest rising and the hips lifting. Let the arms do all of it.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Hips read against the start (body shape alone tilts the line 4–10°); press needs only 20°; one set.
- New check: the legs lifting — a superman photo was counted as a press-up before.

**Sources**

- [Physiopedia — McKenzie method](https://www.physio-pedia.com/McKenzie_Method)

### Knee to chest (`kneechest`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 30 s hold.

**What the guidance asks**

- Lying on the back, both knees bent (or the other leg straight). Bring one knee in with both hands behind the thigh.
- Hold to a stretch (15–30 s), breathing; head and shoulders stay down; the other foot stays down.
- Osteoporosis: ask a physio first (spinal flexion).

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Curling the head and shoulders up | ‘Curling up’ — “Shoulders down on the floor — bring the knee to you”; ‘Head lifting’ — “Rest your head on the floor” |
| The other leg lifting | ‘Other leg lifting’ — “Keep the other foot on the floor” |
| Pulling on the shin or kneecap | *not seen by the camera:* words only |
| Bouncing | *not seen by the camera:* not judged |

**What the camera is held to**

- In the hold, the hip angle (knee–hip–shoulder) must be at most 75°.
- In the hold, the height of the other heel over the hip, as % of the other shin, as the change since the start, must be at most 15%.
- In the hold, the ear over the shoulder (the neck line off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 15°.
- In the hold, the shoulder over the hip (the trunk line off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 8°.

*It cannot see:* How the lower back feels and where the hands hold — behind the thigh, not over the kneecap: it sees how close the knee is, the other foot staying down and the head and shoulders staying on the floor.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Other leg judged on its foot staying down (bent or straight).
- New checks: shoulders curling up and the head lifting; close hug at 75° (60° was nearly full hip bend).
- Osteoporosis caution added.

**Sources**

- [Mayo Clinic — Back exercises](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/back-pain/art-20546859)
- [UPMC / Healthwise — Single knee-to-chest stretch](https://www.upmc.com/)

### Hip hinge (`hinge`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 3 × 10 reps, 2 s at the top, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- Feet hip-width, knees soft; push the hips straight back, the trunk tipping forward with the back long and the head in line.
- About halfway (correct waiter’s bows are 50–70° at the hip); stop where the hamstrings pull or the back would round; stand by driving the hips forward.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Squatting / knees forward | ‘Squatting’ — “Don’t squat — hips back, knees only soft”; ‘Shins tipping’ — “Shins upright — push the hips back” |
| Back rounding, head dropping | ‘Back rounding’ — “Keep the back long — chest and head in line” |
| Looking up | ‘Looking up’ — “Don’t look up — keep the back of the head long” |
| Leaning back to finish | ‘Leaning back’ — “Stand tall — finish with the glutes, don’t lean back” |
| Knees locked | ‘Knees locked’ — “Soften the knees” (during the rep) |
| Low-back rounding under a flat-looking trunk | *not seen by the camera:* words only |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be between 40° and 75°.
- Between reps, the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright must be at least -5°.
- Through the rep, the knee angle (hip–knee–ankle) must be between 150° and 172°.
- Through the rep, the shoulder off the straight line from the ear to the hip, as the change since the start, must be between -15° and 20°.
- Through the rep, the shin line (ankle to knee) from the floor must be at most 105°.
- The rep is under way once the trunk line (hip to shoulder) from upright rises past 25° and counts once it is back under 12°.

*It cannot see:* The lower back rounding a little under a flat-looking trunk: it sees the trunk angle, the knees, the shins and the head dropping. A dowel along the spine tells you what it cannot.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- Facing from the feet, so leaning back can read negative.
- Shins-forward check fixed (it fired only with the knee behind the ankle).
- Back read signed against standing; depth 40–75°; knees judged locked only during the rep, at 172.

**Sources**

- [Luomajoki et al. 2007, movement control tests of the low back (BMC Musculoskeletal Disorders)](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2474-8-90)
- [Physiopedia — Movement control tests for the lumbar spine](https://www.physio-pedia.com/Movement_Control_Tests_for_the_Lumbar_Spine)

### Cat–camel (`catcamel`) — side on to the phone

Default dose: 1 × 8 reps, lowering over 1 s.

**What the guidance asks**

- All fours; round the back up (cat), chin and tailbone tucked, breathing out; let it dip (camel), the head lifting gently.
- Motion, not stretch — no pushing at the ends. McGill: 5–8 cycles are enough.

**Mistakes it names, and what the coach does about each**

| mistake | the coach |
|---|---|
| Moving only the neck | *not seen by the camera:* words only (the head is the beat) |
| Pushing into the end range | *not seen by the camera:* words only |
| Cranking the head up in the dip | ‘Neck cranking’ — “Easy with the neck — let the back dip, the head just follows” |
| Elbows bending | ‘Arms bending’ — “Arms straight, hands under the shoulders” |
| Rocking | ‘Rocking back’ — “Hips over the knees — don’t rock back”; ‘Hips too far forward’ — “Hips over the knees” |

**What the camera is held to**

- At the top of the rep, the ear over the shoulder (the neck line off level), as the change since the start, must be at most -15°.
- At all times, the ear over the shoulder (the neck line off level), as the change since the start, must be at most 30°.
- At all times, the elbow angle (shoulder–elbow–wrist) must be at least 160°.
- Through the rep, the thigh line (knee to hip) from the floor must be between 75° and 105°.
- The rep is under way once the ear over the shoulder (the neck line off level), as the change since the start falls under -10° and counts once it is back over -4°.

*It cannot see:* The curve of the spine itself: it follows your head as the beat of the movement, with the arms and the hips staying over the knees. A nod alone would count — start each curve from the tailbone and let the head follow.

**Changed in the 2026-10 review**

- “Pushing too far” removed (it fired on the head hanging as asked).
- New check: the neck cranking up in the camel; 8 cycles, 1 set; the words say a nod alone would count.

**Sources**

- [McGill — Enhancing low back health through stabilization exercise](https://www.backfitpro.com/)
