# Form standards behind the exercise files

What each exercise in `public/exercises/` asks for, where the standard came
from, and how it was turned into a number the camera can hold someone to.
The numbers are starting points chosen from published guidance; the
Recordings tab tunes them against real takes, and the file is where the
tuned numbers go.

Two things run through all of them:

- **What the camera can measure** is an angle between three landmarks, a
  line against vertical or the floor, or one point's height over another.
  Words like "tighten the thigh" or "keep the hips stacked" have no number,
  so they go into the how-to and into *what the camera cannot see*, not into
  a fault.
- **Order of correction** is the chain of cause: whatever the rest of the
  body stands on is said first. Set-up faults are coached at the start,
  before the movement is asked for.

## Four-way straight leg raises

The classic post-injury quadriceps and hip programme: the same straight-leg
lift done lying on the back (hip flexion), on the side (abduction), face down
(extension) and on the side with the bottom leg (adduction).

### Straight leg raise (`slr`) — lying on the back

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Other knee bent, foot flat, to protect the back | NHS South Tees; Hinge Health | start words; the straighter leg is the one measured |
| Toes pulled back toward you, knee pressed straight before the lift | NHS South Tees ("pull the foot back… press the back of the knee down") | `kneeBend` and `toesDown` are set-up faults: knee ≥ 165°, foot angle at the heel 60–105° |
| Lift the whole leg with the knee straight | NHS South Tees; Hinge Health | `kneeBend` stays on through the rep |
| Height: "no more than 6 inches" (NHS) to "the height of the other knee" (most physio handouts, about 30–45°) | NHS South Tees; Hinge Health; Wikipedia (about 12 inches) | the lift as the hip angle between shoulder and knee, 130–155° at the top, i.e. a lift of 25–50°; too high and too low are both faults. The NHS figure is the conservative early-rehab version; lower `liftMax` toward 165 for that |
| Hold a couple of seconds, lower slowly | NHS South Tees | hold 2 s at the top, "lower slowly" judged at 1 s |

What it cannot see: the lower back flattening, the thigh muscle actually
tightening.

### Side-lying leg raise (`sideraise`) — hip abduction

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Hips stacked, bottom knee bent for balance, top leg straight in line with the body | ACE Exercise Library; Rehab Hero | start words; the higher ankle is the leg measured; knee ≥ 165° |
| Lift to about 45°: the hip abducts no further, past that the pelvis hikes and the work shifts from gluteus medius to TFL | ACE; Joint Ventures PT; Distance-runner EMG study (PMC3418110) | the lift as the angle between trunk and leg, 130–150° at the top (30–50°); higher is a fault |
| Toes forward, not turned up; body not rolling back | ACE; Melbourne CHESM; Monster Longe | not measurable from one view: in *cannot see* and the how-to |
| Slow and controlled, pause at the top | ACE | hold 2 s, lower slowly 1 s |

### Prone leg raise (`proneraise`) — hip extension

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Face down, forehead on the hands, both legs straight | Wellen; Pritikin | start words |
| Lift "about 6 inches or less"; both hip bones stay on the floor | Pritikin; Wellen; MelioGuide | the lift as the angle between trunk and leg, 150–170° (10–30°); too high is a fault |
| Do not arch the back; lift from the hip, not the foot; feel it in the hip not the back | Pritikin; Wellen | the shoulders' rise above the hips within ±12°: past it the chest is coming up and the back arching (`archBack`) |
| Knee straight | Wellen | `kneeBend`, set-up |

### Inner thigh raise (`innerraise`) — hip adduction

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| On the side, hips stacked, top knee bent with the foot flat in front of the bottom knee | PureGym; Rehab Hero; Fit PA | start words; the straighter (bottom) leg is the one measured |
| Bottom leg straight, ankle flexed, lifted from the inner thigh in a controlled arc, not swung | PureGym; Kovo Fitness | `kneeBend` set-up fault; slow lowering judged |
| Lift only as far as the pelvis stays still; "avoid high lifts" | Fit PA; Dani Winks | the lift 150–168° (12–30°); higher is a fault |
| Pause at the top; lower without resting the leg between reps | PureGym; Rehab Hero | hold 2 s; the rest is in the how-to |

## Forward lunge (`lunge`)

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Front knee bent to about 90°, thigh parallel to the floor | Cleveland Clinic; Airrosti; Exakt Health | front knee angle 80–100° at the bottom; `kneeShallow` and `kneeDeep` |
| Front knee stacked over the ankle, not drifting past the toes | Cleveland Clinic; Anytime Fitness; Exakt Health | the front shin's lean forward of vertical at most 20° (`kneeOver`), said first because it comes from the step length |
| Torso upright, shoulders back, no leaning over the front leg | Cleveland Clinic; Airrosti | trunk within ±15° of vertical: `leanFwd`, `leanBack` |
| Back knee hovers just above the floor, bent to about 90° | Cleveland Clinic; Darebee | the back leg is on the far side of the body and cannot be measured from this view; in *cannot see* |
| Push back through the front heel to standing | Cleveland Clinic | the rep counts when the front knee is straight again (past 160°) |

The front leg is picked each frame as the one whose ankle is ahead of the hip.

## Static quads (`quadset`) — quad sets

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Lying with the leg straight, the other knee bent | NHS South Tees; Knee Guru | start words; the straighter leg is measured |
| Pull the toes back toward you and press the back of the knee down; feel the thigh tighten; the heel may lift an inch | NHS South Tees; Knee Guru; PhysioAdvisor | the toes coming up is the marker the camera has: the foot angle at the heel falling below 98° starts the rep, rising past 110° ends it; the knee ≥ 170° while held (`kneeBend`) |
| Hold for a count of five, relax fully, repeat | NHS South Tees; Physiobot | hold 5 s at the top, 3 s quiet after each |
| Frequent, gentle: "every half hour while in bed" in the NHS handout | NHS South Tees | words; no load option |

What it cannot see: the contraction itself. It sees the two things that go
with it. Honest, but it is the least camera-friendly of the eight; a towel
under the knee and a strong "toes up" make it readable.

## Dynamic quads (`kneeext`) — seated knee extension

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Sit tall in a sturdy chair, feet flat, hands on the seat | More Life Health; Rehab Hero | start words; trunk within ±15° of vertical at the start (`leanBack`, `leanFwd` set-up) |
| Straighten the knee fully, "locked out into full extension" | Rehab Hero; Physiobot | knee ≥ 165° at the top (`kneeShort`); the rep is under way past 130° and counts back under 105° |
| Thigh stays supported on the chair: the knee does the lifting, not the hip | More Life Health; Rehab Hero | the knee's rise above the hip within ±12° (`thighLift`) |
| Hold a strong contraction at the top, 5–7 s | Rehab Hero; Physiobot | hold 5 s |
| Toes pulled up (common physio cue) | Physiobot; PhysioAdvisor | foot angle 60–105°, optional reading (`toesDown`) |

## Step-up (`stepup`)

| standard | source | in the file |
|---|---|---|
| Step height: knee at about 90° with the foot on the step, no higher than the knee; start low | Prehab Guys; Walton Surgery; Samarpan Physio | words; the rep's return is counted at a knee angle under 135° so a low step still counts |
| Whole lead foot on the step; knee tracks over the second and third toes | Prehab Guys; Home Physiotherapy Exercises | tracking inward is not visible side-on; the shin's lean forward of the ankle at most 30° at the start (`kneeOver`) catches a foot placed too far back or a step too high |
| Trunk upright, chest up, centre of mass over the box; no throwing the trunk at the step | Prehab Guys; Mobile Physiotherapy Clinic | trunk within ±20° of vertical (`leanFwd` set-up, `leanBack`) |
| Stand up fully at the top without jumping off the back leg | Prehab Guys; Walton Surgery | knee ≥ 165° and hip ≥ 160° at the top (`kneeShort`, `hipBent`) |
| Control the step down; no momentum | Prehab Guys; Samarpan Physio | "lower slowly" judged at 1 s |

The leg on the step is picked each frame as the one whose thigh is raised.
