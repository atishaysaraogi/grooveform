    What it measures

    Knee angle — the angle at your knee between hip and ankle. 90° is thighs parallel to the floor. Above the band your legs are too straight, below it you are too deep.

    Shin — the angle the line from your knee to your heel makes with the floor. Plumb is 90° and the band is 85–95°. Past 95° your heels are ahead of your knees and your feet come back; under 85° they are behind your knees and your feet go forward.

    What gets said first — feet, then knee, then back, whichever is further out. That order is the chain of cause: where your feet are decides which knee angles you can reach, and where your knee is decides what your back has to do to balance it. Being told to flatten a back that is only leaning because your feet are wrong asks for something the stance will not give.

    The set — sixty seconds, counted down from the moment the position is right. Standing up pauses the clock rather than running it down, so sixty seconds means sixty seconds of wall sit. The time is called out at 45, 30, 10 and 5 seconds left.

    Back — how far the line from your hip to your shoulder leans off vertical. Against a wall it should be straight up. The camera cannot see your spine rounding between those two points; no pose model gives a mid-spine point, so this is the tilt and nothing more.
