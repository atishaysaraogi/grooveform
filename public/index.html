The camera never leaves this device.

Put the phone on the floor a couple of metres away, the way round this exercise asks for, then step into the frame. The set starts as soon as the camera does, the picture takes the whole screen once the phone is the right way round, and the cues are spoken out loud. The video you download has the sound the microphone heard, cues included.

Nearly every phone hands over the picture the way you are holding it, so leave this alone. If a standing exercise still comes up wide with the phone upright, try Flip camera first: the front and back cameras do not always behave the same. It is here for the few that give the camera's own frame whatever way the phone is held, which arrives as a body lying on its side; a quarter turn puts it back, and if it comes out upside down take the other one.

These are holds, not fast movements, so the full model is worth its cost: it is the steadier read, and steadier readings are what stop a cue firing on nothing.

    What it measures

    Wall sit — the angle at your knee between hip and ankle, which should sit in 85–110°; the angle your shin makes with the floor, plumb at 90° and allowed 85–95°, where past 95° your heels are ahead of your knees and under 85° they are behind them; and how far the line from your hip to your shoulder leans off vertical, allowed ±12°.

    Elbow plank — where your shoulder sits over your elbow, measured as the lean of your upper arm off vertical, allowed from 5° behind to 15° in front; and how far your hip sits off the straight line from shoulder to ankle, allowed ±5°. Above the line your hips are piked and are told to come down, below it they are sagging and are told to lift.

    Knee raise — standing side on, tall, one knee up: the angle at that knee between hip and ankle, and the angle at its heel between the toe and the knee, the first allowed 80–100° and the second 60–100°. Hold ten seconds, lower slowly, and the rep counts when you are back to standing. Ten reps. The hold and the number of reps are both settings. How far the thigh has to come up to count as raised is a setting, not a mark — it is what tells a raise from standing still, so that the two right angles are the only things being judged.

    Glute bridge — lying on your back side on, knees bent, feet flat, phone on its side: the shin's angle at the heel between toe and knee, allowed 85–110°, which is where your feet are (over it the feet are out too far and are walked in, under it too close); the angle at the hip between knee and shoulder, at least 160° at the top, which is the lift; how far the hip rises above the knee, at most 3°, because the hips are not to go higher than the knees; and the foot's line off the floor, ±10°, because the heels and toes stay down — and that is checked first, then the shin, then the hip. Hold two seconds at the top, lower slowly — a way down that takes under a second is remarked on with the count — and the rep counts when the hips are back down. Ten reps.

    Donkey kick — on hands and knees, side on, phone on its side: the arms plumb from the floor, 85–105°, with the elbows straight (at least 165°); the back level, hip to shoulder within ±10° of the floor; the working knee kept at a right angle, 80–100°; and the lift, the angle at the hip between knee and shoulder, at least 160° at the top — the thigh in line with the back — and no more than 5° above that line, because past it the lower back is arching to make height. Hold two seconds at the top, lower slowly, and the rep counts when the knee is back down. Ten reps, one leg per set, the sets alternating legs. The app follows whichever leg is lifted, so kick with the leg nearest the camera.

    What gets said first — the fault earliest in the chain of cause, not the one furthest out. For the wall sit that is feet, then knee, then back; for the plank, shoulders, then hips; for the bridge, the feet flat on the floor, then where they are, both before the lift is even asked for, then the hips; for the donkey kick, the hands and arms, then the back they carry, then the working leg. Where your feet or your elbows are decides what the rest of you can do, so a correction further along the chain asks for something the base will not give.

    The set — for the holds, sixty seconds counted down from the moment the position is right; for the knee raise, ten seconds a rep and ten reps. Coming out of it pauses the clock rather than running it down, so sixty seconds means sixty seconds of the exercise. The time is called at 45, 30, 10 and 5 seconds left.

    The camera cannot see your spine rounding between hip and shoulder; no pose model gives a mid-spine point, so every move judges that line by its ends and nothing more.
